Lean = the team with the better offensive spot (higher score). Spreads/totals recompute live when you change the weight sliders on the Slate tab.
Park = run environment of the home stadium (only extreme parks tagged). In the backtest it moves the total most when combined is high (≥120: hitter park 68% over 8.5 vs 56% at a pitcher park) or low; it washes out in the middle. Over = hitter-friendly, Under = pitcher-friendly. "~wind" = Wrigley, where wind dominates and the tag is only a season average.
Reading this tab vs the Slate tab: Quality here is how good the pen itself is (100 = elite). On the Slate tab, the Opp BP sub-score is the opposite view — it grades the pen your offense is facing, so a high number there means the opponent's pen is weak. Roughly, Slate Opp BP ≈ 100 − opponent's Quality here.
Effective quality = 50% season + 30% last-14 + 20% last-7, each scored on ERA + WHIP + K-BB% + HR/9 and mapped to the same 2.50–6.50 axis the model already uses (recentred each run so a league-average pen keeps its own season ERA; only recent form moves it). Those weights were picked by backtest over 2024, 2025 and 2026; mixing time windows beat season ERA alone in all three seasons (r vs runs 0.089 → 0.122).
3-day workload and arms-down are shown but carry zero weight. They were tested and add nothing — consistent with the 4-season finding that bullpen fatigue does not predict runs allowed. Read them for the late innings, not as a betting input.
L7 is 15–25 innings. One bad outing swings it two runs. That is why it gets 20% and not more.
